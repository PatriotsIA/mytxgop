Re: Request to extend the Election Code § 127.201 manual count to every precinct for the U.S. Senate race and report the reconciliation before the state canvass

Dear Judge [Name]:

I write as Chairman of the [County] County Republican Party to ask for one narrow thing: that [County] County count by hand the paper ballots cast in the United States Senate race, compare the hand count to the totals reported by our voting machines, and report the result to the Secretary of State before the state canvass.

Texas law already requires a partial manual count after every election that uses an electronic voting system. Under Election Code § 127.201 the general custodian of election records counts the paper in at least one percent of precincts, or three, whichever is greater, beginning within 72 hours of the polls closing and finishing by the 21st day after the election. Nothing in the Code limits a county to the minimum. We are asking that for one race — the one that is identical on every ballot in Texas — the count cover every precinct in the county rather than a sample. The manual count runs on its own schedule and does not delay the local canvass; a county that has already canvassed can still extend the count and report the reconciliation before the state canvass.

The reasons are the ones you have already heard this year from the federal government. In July the Department of Homeland Security conditioned election-security grant funding on a post-election manual audit of at least five percent of paper ballots in federal elections and a transition away from barcode and QR-code ballots. In August and September the Secretary of Homeland Security wrote that voting equipment in use across the country contains components manufactured in adversary nations, and that election security is national security. Counting the paper in one race is the simplest way to answer those concerns for our county with our own ballots.

I want to be plain about what this is and is not. It is not a request to delay or withhold certification; please canvass on schedule. It is not an allegation that any result in [County] County was wrong. It is not a challenge to any provision of the Election Code. If the paper matches the machines, the result stands and the argument is over here. If it does not, every voter in this county would want to know, whichever candidate it helps.

The [County] County Republican Party will supply trained volunteers for the count, under the supervision of your election officials and with observers from both parties present, at no cost to the county. I would be glad to meet with you and the general custodian at your convenience to work out the details, and I would appreciate a response by [date], so that I can report which counties have agreed.

Thank you for your service to [County] County.
